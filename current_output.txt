What is Divorce?

Divorce, in the legal context of marriage, refers to the formal dissolution of a marriage by a
court or other competent body. It legally ends the marital union, releasing both parties from
all  obligations  and  rights  associated  with  it.  The  process  requires  specific  grounds  and
adherence  to  legal  procedures,  often  involving  significant  emotional  and  financial
considerations for all parties involved.

Why is Divorce required?

Divorce  becomes  a  necessary  legal  recourse  when  a  marriage  has  irretrievably  broken
down, and reconciliation is not possible or desired by one or both parties. It is required to
legally  terminate  marital  ties,  allowing  individuals  to  remarry,  and  to  provide  legal
frameworks for resolving issues such as child custody, alimony, maintenance, and division
of property that arise from the dissolution of the marriage.

Documents Required for Divorce

To  initiate  and  proceed  with  a  divorce,  several  key  documents  are  typically  required.
These include the Petition for Divorce, which formally requests the dissolution of marriage
and  outlines  the  grounds.  An  Affidavit  in  support  of  the  Petition  is  needed  to  affirm  the
truthfulness  of  the  statements  made  under  oath.  Proof  of  valid  marriage,  usually  in  the
form of a Marriage Certificate, is essential. Proof of residence, such as an Aadhaar card or
Voter  ID,  is  necessary  to  establish  the  court's  jurisdiction.  Evidence  substantiating  the
grounds for divorce, like medical reports or communication records, is critical. If alimony,
maintenance,  or  property  division  is  sought,  relevant  Financial  Documents  (income
statements,  bank  statements,  property  deeds)  must  be  provided.  For  cases  involving
minor  children,  Child  Custody  related  documents  are  paramount.  Lastly,  copies  of  any
previous legal proceedings related to the marriage may be required.

Procedure for Divorce

The procedure for divorce generally begins with Step 1: Consulting a lawyer to understand
the grounds and prepare the Petition for Divorce, which takes about 1 to 7 days and costs
approximately Rs.5,000 to Rs.15,000. Following this, Step 2 involves filing the Petition and
supporting  documents  in  the  appropriate  Family  Court,  a  process  that  takes  1  to  3  days
and  incurs  court  fees  of  Rs.300  to  Rs.1,000.  Step  3  is  the  court  issuing  a  notice  to  the
respondent, who must file a response within 30 to 90 days, with associated costs for court
fees  and  appearance.  Step  4  involves  Mediation  or  Conciliation  proceedings  to  explore
settlement, typically lasting 15 to 60 days, with costs ranging from Rs.1,000 to Rs.5,000. If
no settlement is reached, Step 5 requires parties to submit evidence, including documents
and  witness  testimonies,  which  can  take  3  months  to  1.5  years  and  involve  significant
legal  fees.  Step  6  entails  the  court  hearing  final  arguments  from  both  sides,  scheduled
over 1 to 4 weeks, with associated legal costs. Step 7 is the court pronouncing judgment
and  issuing  the  Decree  of  Divorce,  usually  within  1  to  4  weeks  after  final  arguments,
costing  Rs.100  to  Rs.500  for  a  certified  copy.  Finally,  Step  8  allows  for  an  appeal  to  a
higher court if a party is unsatisfied, a process that can take 6 months to 2 years and incur
substantial legal and court fees.

Legal Considerations for Divorce

Several  legal  considerations  are  paramount  when  navigating  a  divorce.  The  applicable
personal laws, such as the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, or the Special Marriage Act, 1954,
dictate  the  grounds  for  divorce,  procedural  requirements,  and  provisions  for  alimony,
maintenance,  and  child  custody.  Establishing  the  jurisdiction  of  the  court  is  crucial,
typically  based  on  the  place  of  marriage  or  residence.  The  concept  of  'irretrievable
breakdown of marriage' is increasingly recognized, although specific grounds must still be
met under most statutes. For mutual consent divorces, a cooling-off period and joint filing
are  usually  required.  Issues  concerning  child  custody  are  decided  based  on  the  child's

welfare,  while  alimony  and  maintenance  are  determined  considering  the  needs  of  the
recipient  spouse  and  the  capacity  of  the  other  spouse.  Property  division  follows  specific
legal principles, aiming for equitable distribution.
