| Aspect | Kerala | Karnataka |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Core school‑education law | Kerala Education Act & Kerala Education Rules (KER), plus state RTE Rules implementing the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009. | Karnataka Education Act, 1983 and Karnataka RTE Rules, along with more recent State Education Policy documents aligning (in part) with NEP 2020.|
| Mandatory state language as **subject** | **Malayalam Language Learning Act, 2017** makes Malayalam **compulsory as a subject** from Class 1 to 10 in all schools (state, CBSE, CISCE, etc.). | Kannada is compulsory as a **language subject** in schools in Karnataka; multiple circulars and policies require Kannada to be taught even in CBSE/CISCE schools.|
| Push to make state language **first language** | The 2025 Malayalam Language Bill aims to make Malayalam the **first language** in Kerala schools, with limited exceptions for linguistic minorities; this is explicitly framed as a response to Hindi‑imposition debates. | Karnataka policy debates focus on ensuring **Kannada is at least one compulsory language** and preferably the medium up to Class 5, but the state has recently reaffirmed a **two‑language formula** (Kannada/mother tongue + English), not a strict “Kannada‑first everywhere” mandate. |
| Medium of instruction (primary classes) | Kerala Education Rules allow English or Malayalam as medium; they also protect existing facilities and allow other languages (e.g., Tamil, Kannada) as medium in notified linguistic‑minority areas. | State policy recommends Kannada/mother tongue as **medium up to Class 5**, with English typically introduced early as a second language; the state education policy and circulars emphasise Kannada‑medium schooling, especially in government schools. |
| Treatment of linguistic minorities | KER explicitly protects pre‑existing minority‑language schools and allows managements to continue using their minority language as medium, while still mandating Malayalam as a subject.  | Karnataka recognises minority institutions, but language policy (Kannada‑compulsory) has led to litigation/controversy with some Urdu/Tamil/English‑medium schools and parent groups over compulsory Kannada in CBSE/CISCE schools. |
| RTE 25% reservation – legal position | Both follow the Supreme Court ruling in *Society for Unaided Private Schools v. Union of India*; Kerala has issued specific Government Orders and created RTE cells for implementation, but with relatively lower conflict compared to some Hindi‑belt states. | Karnataka is one of the **test‑case states** for RTE 25% implementation; there have been extensive litigations and administrative circulars on neighbourhood criteria, reimbursement rates, and private‑school obligations. |
| State vs NEP 2020 stance | Kerala has been **openly critical of parts of NEP 2020**, framing its own state‑level policy that stresses secular, public‑education strengthening and strong role for Malayalam.| Karnataka has been **more receptive to NEP 2020**, rolling out state‑level implementation (especially in higher education) and aligning school language policy (mother‑tongue/Kannada + English) with NEP’s three‑language formula, but retaining its two‑language formula in practice.|
