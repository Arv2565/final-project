| Aspect | Kerala | Karnataka |
|------|------|------|
| Main statute | Abkari Act, 1 of 1077 (Malayalam Era) and allied Abkari/Foreign Liquor Rules; annual “Abkari Policy” notifies bar policy. | Karnataka Excise Act, 1965 and Karnataka Excise Rules (e.g., General Conditions of Licences, retail and bar licence rules), plus yearly excise policy. |
| Legal drinking age | **23 years** – Section 15A Abkari Act; age raised from 21 to 23 by 2017–21 amendments/ordinance and now reflected in state policy. | Practically **21 years** – Excise Act prohibits sale to <18, but Excise Licence Rules bar sale to persons below 21; proposal to reduce to 18 was withdrawn after public opposition, so enforcement remains at 21. |
| Retail structure | Near-complete **state monopoly**: Kerala State Beverages (Manufacturing & Marketing) Corporation (BEVCO/KSBC) controls wholesale and most retail IMFL and beer outlets; private bars only serve for on-premise consumption. | Mixed system: Karnataka State Beverages Corporation manages wholesale for most IMFL, but retail is via privately run CL-2 (off-shop), CL-9 (bar & restaurant) and other licences; no BEVCO-style retail monopoly. |
| Bar-licence policy | Very restrictive historically: 2014–15 policy closed all bar hotels below 5-star; Supreme Court upheld Kerala’s decision to confine FL-3 licences mainly to 5-star hotels (later partially liberalised to 3-star & above). Government oscillates between tightening and modest liberalisation but keeps a public-health framing. | More liberal: large number of CL-9 bar licences across the state; policy focus is revenue and orderliness rather than step-by-step prohibition. New rules and e-auctions periodically add/redistribute bar licences. |
| Policy stance (prohibition vs control) | Official line: **no total prohibition**, but “abstinence and reduction of consumption”; uses high prices, state monopoly, and tight bar rules rather than banning alcohol outright. | Explicitly **non-prohibitionist**; focus on maximising regulated revenue from excise duty and licence fees, while occasionally tweaking age limits and hours to address law-and-order concerns. |
| Competition / market structure issues | KSBC/BEVCO repeatedly accused of abusing dominant position in procurement and retail pricing; complaints before CCI and NCLAT argue it fixes low purchase prices and prefers government brands, reflecting its monopoly power. | Multiple private licensees and distributors reduce pure monopoly issues; competition concerns in Karnataka liquor market relate more to cartelisation/tendering, not a single state-retail monopoly like Kerala. |
