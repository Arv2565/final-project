| Aspect | Kerala | Karnataka |
|------|------|------|
| Key inland fisheries law | **Kerala Inland Fisheries and Aquaculture Act, 2010** – codifies and updates all inland-fishery laws; focuses on sustainable development, conservation, social fisheries, and regulation of aquaculture. | **Karnataka Inland Fisheries (Conservation, Development and Regulation) Act, 1996** – consolidates and regulates inland fisheries, including conservation, leasing of fishery rights and constitution of an Inland Fisheries Advisory Board. |
| Inland aquaculture & public water bodies | 2010 Act lets government notify public water bodies as **aquaculture areas**, regulate introduction of non-indigenous species, ban destructive practices, declare fish sanctuaries, and involve local bodies in management; strong emphasis on protecting traditional fisher livelihoods. | 1996 Act regulates **leasing of fishery rights** in tanks, reservoirs, etc., registration of hatcheries, declaration of sanctuaries, and formation of an advisory board; more oriented to structured leasing and development through co-ops and private players. |
| Cooperatives / social fisheries | Kerala Act explicitly mentions “social fisheries” and traditional rights; many schemes route benefits through coastal and inland fisher cooperatives, but statute stresses livelihood and ecological protection. | Karnataka leans heavily on **fishermen co-operative societies** (over 600 active, 2.04 lakh members) and a state-level federation; reservoir fisheries and new cage-aquaculture projects are often implemented via co-ops and PPP-type models. |
| Marine fishing regulation – core statute | **Kerala Marine Fishing Regulation Act, 1980** – provides for zoning, mesh-size limits, and restrictions on mechanised fishing to protect traditional fishers. | **Karnataka Marine Fishing (Regulation) Act, 1986** – similar state law for zoning, licensing and banning destructive gear along Karnataka coast. |
| Monsoon trawl ban | Kerala was a **pioneer**: introduced a state-level monsoon trawl ban in 1988; now typically enforces a **52-day ban** on mechanised trawling during early monsoon to protect spawning. | Karnataka also has a monsoon trawl ban but with **shorter duration** (around 45–61 days depending on year) and slightly different dates; implementation details (dates, permitted crafts) differ from Kerala’s regime. |
| Regulation of gear & destructive methods | Kerala’s 1980 Act and rules reserve inshore zones for traditional craft, restrict trawler operations close to shore and impose mesh-size limits; deep-sea trawling and foreign/industrial trawlers have been politically sensitive (e.g., EMCC MoU controversy). | Karnataka increasingly bans **unscientific methods** like bull trawling and light fishing under the 1986 Act; recent Udupi orders (backed by HC) ban pair trawling and light-based fishing, with penalties including diesel cut-off and cancellation of registration. |
| Stance on Centre’s deep-sea/EEZ policy | Kerala is vocally critical of the Union government’s latest **deep-sea fishing/EEZ draft rules**, arguing they favour industrial fleets and recentralise power at the cost of state laws and traditional fishers; seeks a Parliamentary law with strong small-scale-fisher protections. | Karnataka has not opposed the EEZ-draft framework as strongly in public; its coastal politics are more focused on controlling destructive gear and expanding production (including through inland cage aquaculture and reservoirs) rather than directly confronting the Centre’s EEZ regime. |
